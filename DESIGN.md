# Design System: Tiny Dreams

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "A biblioteca dos pequenos feitiços"**

A biblioteca dos pequenos feitiços is a nocturnal literary garden: moonlit plum, warm paper, copper light, illustrated potion glass, pumpkins, and friendly ghosts. Handmade richness belongs in the working components as well as the scenery; clear labels, readable progress, and generous controls keep the daily ritual practical.

Literature is permanent; October’s Halloween appearance is seasonal. Twelve prepared editions span October 2026 through September 2027, selected automatically by the São Paulo calendar. Their compositions, objects, materials, light, and ambient effects change substantially within a cozy, handmade literary refuge. Future worlds remain surprises unless the user explicitly opens a preview; creating editions beyond the finite schedule requires new authored work.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Handmade seasonal illustration within a permanent literary identity.
- Bookish display typography paired with plain, readable controls.
- Tonal cards and restrained light, with progress expressed through illustrated talismans.

## Colors

October combines moonlit plum and paper with copper light and quiet glass tones. The unprefixed frontmatter colors preserve that base system; date-prefixed tokens record exact seasonal overrides from `web/ui/editions.json`. `bg`, `surface`, `text`, `muted`, `line`, and `accent` map to the same CSS variables; the three `goal-*` colors map to the persistent goal color slots. Their object names and appearance change with the edition, while goal identity does not.

April, May, and September use light paper palettes with dark ink. Their hero copy sits on an opaque `surface`, including its date, heading, subtitle, and creation action. Paper primary actions use `goal-lavender` with `surface` text; functional links retain readable ink. Other editions retain pale scenic hero text and dark working surfaces. Do not reuse October’s dark field or secondary-button literals in seasonal variants: these inherit the selected background, surface, text, and line.

### Primary
- **Copper candlelight** (`accent`): primary actions, active navigation, focus, and calendar markers.

### Secondary
- **Lavender potion** (`lavender`): default goal progress and talisman.
- **Pumpkin lantern** (`peach`): warm goal progress and pumpkin talisman.
- **Sage glass** (`sage`): cool goal progress and friendly ghost talisman.
- **Rose warning** (`danger`): destructive confirmation, paired with dark ink.

### Neutral
- **Moonlit plum** (`bg`): continuous page background.
- **Library shadow** (`surface`): goal cards; peach and sage cards receive subtly tinted backgrounds in the stylesheet.
- **Paper ivory** (`text`): primary text; **lavender dust** (`muted`) supports metadata.
- **Shelf edge** (`line`): navigation and list divisions.
- **Field shadow** (`field`, `field-border`): inset form fields with a readable boundary.

## Typography

**Display Font:** locally hosted Fraunces with Georgia and serif fallbacks.
**Body Font:** locally hosted DM Sans with a sans-serif fallback.

Fraunces gives headings and counts the warmth of a storybook; DM Sans makes daily operations direct. Assets are `web/assets/fraunces.ttf` and `web/assets/dm-sans.ttf`, with license files alongside them.

Display, title, body, and label roles are normative in the frontmatter. At the phone breakpoint the display becomes (46px), with line height (1.12) and tracking (-1.6px); goal titles become (25px). Countdown numerals use Fraunces (54px), increasing to (55px) on phones, with tabular numerals. Form fields use (14px) on desktop and (16px) on phones to avoid iOS focus zoom. Uppercase reward labels use tracking (1.7px).

**The Literary World Rule.** Every seasonal world must connect to literature; its appearance and metaphor may change while personal records remain intact. The permanent emotional anchor is a cozy library: intimate, welcoming, bookish, and handmade. Monthly editions must vary composition and illustrated elements substantially, not merely recolor the current template. Lighting, materials, objects, and celebrations can change freely within this anchor; everyday controls remain understandable. Follow Brazilian autumn, winter, and spring, without summer-themed editions; preserve Halloween in October and Christmas in December.

## Layout

The top bar has a centered maximum width (1280px); main content has a maximum width (1192px) with horizontal padding (28px). The desktop goal grid has three equal columns and gaps (20px); at (850px) it becomes two columns, and at (560px) one column with gaps (18px). Phone page padding is (22px). The scenic welcome occupies approximately (405px) on desktop and (395px) on phones.

At the phone breakpoint the edition label disappears, tabs share the full width, and form rows stack. Scattered decorative phrases and the handwritten moon note are removed across sizes. Dialog width is the smaller of (510px) or viewport width minus (32px), with maximum height (90dvh) and internal scrolling. Toast positioning accounts for the bottom safe area. Preserve the breathing room between scenic entry and usable workspace without prescribing this home composition to every future screen.

## Elevation & Depth

Depth comes primarily from painted scenery and tonal surfaces. Cards remain flat; the progress fill carries a small shadow. Dialogs and toasts lift above the page, and the modal backdrop blurs the world behind it. Exact shadows and timing are recorded in the sidecar.

**The Honest Light Rule.** Illustrated charge follows total practice progress; decorative light never substitutes for numeric progress or reward status.

## Shapes

Soft rectangular buttons and fields sit within more rounded cards and dialogs. Icon buttons are circular. Progress tracks are slim (5px), with a small bright endpoint; dashed outlines distinguish the add-goal tile. Final dialog styling removes the earlier border, retaining tonal separation and shadow.

## Components

### Buttons

Warm, compact, and clearly labeled. Primary, secondary, and destructive variants use the frontmatter assignments. Primary and secondary controls have minimum height (44px). Primary hover lightens the surface and lifts it (1px); secondary hover lightens its plum fill. Global keyboard focus is a copper outline (2px) offset by (5px). Disabled controls use opacity (.55) and a waiting cursor.

### Inputs / Fields

Dark inset fields have minimum height (46px), a quiet border, explicit labels, and the shared focus outline. Phone fields retain (16px) text and a single column. Errors appear as text in a live alert; placeholders do not replace labels.

### Navigation

Three plain text tabs lead to dreams, today, and calendar. Muted inactive labels become paper ivory when selected, with a copper underline and `aria-current`. Counts use small rounded badges; the small notification dot indicates an outstanding daily action. The badge is informational rather than a selectable chip.

### Cards / Containers

Goal cards use the frontmatter card radius and padding. Each carries title, total practice, streak metadata, remaining days, numeric progress, explicit reward, and check-in or redemption action. The full-width check-in button has an outlined resting state, tinted completed state, and solid earned state; phone height is at least (46px). Long titles and reward names wrap.

### Illustrated talismans

`web/assets/garden.png` supplies the scenic library garden. `web/assets/talismans.png` is the shared three-object image strip, served from `/talismans.png`; current CSS uses background size (auto 100%), scaling uniformly to preserve the original illustration proportions and left/center/right positions for potion, pumpkin, and ghost. The empty state uses the ghost. Keep these objects decorative to assistive technology while retaining textual status.

In October, talisman brightness and saturation increase with the clamped ratio of total check-ins to target. A check-in briefly illuminates the talisman and reward, with a small gift-icon response and fading reward particles. Ambient motion includes occasional shooting stars, nine subtle fireflies, a gently stirring potion with bubbles, and a slowly floating ghost. Art motion uses transforms and preserves illustration proportions. Canvas drawing is capped at 30fps and pixel density 2; there is no sound. All nonessential loops pause offscreen, when the page is hidden, and while dialogs are open. Preferences include a device-local persistent pause switch. Reduced-motion preferences disable spatial animation and canvas; the check-in still retains visible text and color feedback. See `web/assets/PROVENANCE.md` for asset provenance.

Future editions use one illustrated PNG kit per month in `web/assets/editions/`, combining a scene and three objects. The catalog’s measured source geometry and actual transparent gaps define `--scene-ratio`, `--object-aspect`, and `--object-sheet-height`; scale uniformly and preserve natural proportions. The selected kit alone supplies both scenery and object windows. Future objects do not inherit October’s brightness progression or looping potion/ghost transforms: check-ins use a brief 0.9s chapter reveal plus the 1.3s reward bloom. Ambient canvas effects vary among bubbles, rain, petals, stars, and soft drifting points, sharing the same visibility, dialog, device-local pause, and reduced-motion rules.

### Calendar and feedback

The calendar uses seven columns, a copper current-day outline, tinted recorded days, and small check-in dots. Selecting a goal enables past-day correction; future days remain unavailable for recording. Toasts use warm paper and dark ink; dialogs use native modal behavior. Separate, labeled demonstration data supports exploration without pretending to be personal history.

### Automatic calendar and surprise previews

`web/ui/theme-clock.mjs` selects the month in `America/Sao_Paulo`, within the authored range `2026-10`–`2027-09`. Earlier dates use October; later dates retain September until new editions are authored. An active tab checks every 30 seconds and refreshes on visibility restoration. On January 12, 2027, only the real January edition’s heading/subtitle change for the birthday; previews suppress this variation.

Preferences contain closed-by-default “Edições guardadas” details. Opening a preview requires an explicit action, displays a status banner and return controls, and lasts only in memory: reload returns to the actual month. Previewing affects presentation only; it never changes practice dates, goals, check-ins, totals, rewards, or D1 records. Do not preload future artwork. Catalog labels may be present in the closed selector; surprise imagery must remain unloaded until selected. Do not send future artwork or screenshots in the main chat.

Current evidence covers all twelve editions at 320/390/768/1440px, form and tab behavior, state preservation, preview reset, no future-art preloading, schedule/birthday boundaries, and reduced motion. A private fresh review examined 24 desktop/mobile captures; three mobile/contrast findings were corrected and the follow-up verdict passed at fix-list scope. Source checks cover authorization/backend behavior and 4.5:1 text/muted/goal-label token contrast. These checks used an offline QA server with in-memory demonstration goals and made no real D1 writes. Physical iPhone Safari remains unverified. The local app can connect to its dedicated D1 database; the prepared editions remain local and unpublished.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** connect every future world to literature and carry its character into components and interactions.
- **Do** preserve goal identity, check-in dates, total progress, targets, and redemption state when changing a theme.
- **Do** keep rewards, remaining practice days, and undo actions explicit; total progress survives missed days.
- **Do** keep demonstrations labeled and separate from personal records.
- **Do** retain visible state with reduced motion and legible mobile form controls.

### Don't:
- **Don't** replace rich handmade seasonal artwork with generic flat symbols alone.
- **Don't** make a streak the measure of total earned practice.
- **Don't** imply that new editions are generated automatically beyond September 2027.
- **Don't** preload future artwork, reveal surprise images in main chat, or open previews without explicit action.
- **Don't** add scattered decorative phrases; retain headings, subtitles, instructions, status, and data.
- **Don't** let illustration obscure task labels, progress, reward names, or controls.

## Custom habit plans
Keep the dream overview as the default. Reuse existing seasonal surfaces and talismans for habit, list, and amount progress. Cards show schedule, optional deadline, accumulated progress in its own unit, optional reward, and an explicit action opening the routine/list/history. The Today view includes scheduled habits and outstanding dated steps due by today. Only simple habits use direct calendar day toggles; tasks, books, and contributions are corrected in their detailed history.

The creation dialog opens directly as a custom form, without a template selector or the user’s personal examples. Type-specific fields reveal weekday checkboxes and repeatable tasks, individually dated list items, or a numeric target and unit. Start, deadline, notes, and reward are optional. The native dialog scrolls on phones, all fields have labels, and hidden type fields do not participate in validation. Amount recording has its own dated entry form and reversible ledger. Routine recording offers a date selector, individual task controls, total practice, streak, and completed-day history. Existing recorded goals retain their tracking type to keep their history meaningful.

### Quantity-based habits
Within the habit form, “Quando o dia fica feito?” reveals an optional daily quantity and free-text unit. The main target still counts completed practice days. Quantity habits show today's numeric progress on the overview and Today, and open a dated contribution form instead of a manual done button. History offers removable contributions and a per-day progress bar; completion follows the threshold automatically. Task controls are hidden and disabled in this mode. Units stay consistent within a habit with recorded quantities.

### Compact task editor
The creation form groups weekdays and completion rules within “Sua rotina”. Three explicit modes separate a manual daily check, scheduled tasks, and daily quantities. Only the selected mode's fields are visible and participate in validation. Task details use a native accordion: the collapsed summary shows the name and weekday schedule, while the editor provides name, a seven-column weekday selector, remove, and “Pronto”. New tasks inherit the routine weekdays. A live coverage hint identifies selected routine days with no task. Saved task routines reopen in task mode, preserving their entries and dates.

### Dream symbols
Use the original monochrome thin-stroke SVG icon style, with rounded caps, 1.5px strokes, and currentColor. “Símbolo do sonho” offers sixteen icon-only choices with accessible labels in a four-column grid, selected preview, and pressed state. The same SVG appears in the card's upper-left symbol position and Today. Native emoji are internal compatibility keys only, not rendered UI. Illustrated amulets remain automatic and independent of symbol choice.

### Dated habit countdown
A habit deadline owns the card's prominent countdown: calendar days until the end date, labeled “dias até o prazo”. The date row shows the date without a second competing number. Progress remains completed days out of all scheduled “dias do período”; past missed dates never appear as time remaining. Count both today and the deadline date. On the deadline date show “1 dia · termina hoje”, and after it “Prazo encerrado”. Only actual target completion shows the celebration state. Undated habits retain the practice-day countdown.

### Impulse and past-day recording
The habit completion selector adds “Resistir a um impulso”, revealing an optional personal agreement. Its detail view separates “Uma vontade de cada vez” (a dated resisted-urge record plus optional note) from “Como foi o dia?” (success/failure/unrecorded). Use plain, supportive copy: setbacks preserve prior successes; moment records do not imply whole-day success. Keep the selected date explicit.

Daily history uses a pending/all filter, 14-row pagination, dated status labels, direct success/failure actions for simple/impulse habits, and a date-editor link. Task and quantity histories open their existing controls for success. New past-start dreams open the pending history automatically, and cards retain a backfill link. No outcome is assumed for missing dates. Current-day explicit failure ends a streak; completed practice remains. Calendar uses filled points for successes, hollow squares for failures, and dotted points for partial entries, with text/ARIA labels and explicit date editing instead of a toggle that loses the distinction.

### Front-facing daily results
Daily result actions form a two-column group directly on each habit card and in Today. Keep failure equally discoverable, with a thin cross icon and explicit wording. Selected states offer undo and support switching between success and failure without opening an overlay. Detail/history remains a secondary link. For measured or task habits, the success action opens the required controls; the failure action stays visible and is disabled when their completed evidence would contradict it. On phones, Today places the pair on its own full-width row.

### Diving ghost and renewed success feedback
October's sage illustration uses the generated `ghost-jar-v2.png` and `ghost-spirit-v2.png` layers. A stationary 2:3 cloche scene contains a clipped interior, an independently moving spirit, and a duplicate lower foreground for occlusion behind real illustrated plants. The ten-second dive has a short surface pause, a gentle rotated descent, a pause behind the flowers, and an eased resurfacing. Two small bubbles follow a slower eight-second loop. The composite keeps the existing grayscale/brightness ratio from earned progress. Other editions hide the layers and do not reference their image URLs.

The pumpkin gains a restrained lantern sway; the existing potion stir remains. A single success moment animates the progress fill, talisman and reward. The ghost responds with a short cheerful turn, then resumes its dive. A timer removes celebration state even on cards with no reward. Updates from a modal queue until it closes; a regression cancels queued feedback. All ambient loops pause outside the viewport, behind open dialogs, while hidden, or when disabled by the user. Reduced motion shows the spirit in a stable surface pose and suppresses spatial celebrations. Current-page drawing remains capped at 30fps; no sound, dependencies or extra data requests are added by motion.

## Completion library across editions
The Prateleira tab contains completed tydreams as upright book covers and a record beneath each cover: title, earned practice, completion date, optional reward, redemption and history. Active dreams remain in the overview. The shelf uses a three/two/one-column responsive layout, warm shelf edges and active edition surface/text/accent tokens. Existing object celebrations remain; seasonal success variants use the active atmosphere without requesting other artwork. Every edition shares the book interaction, motion pause, reduced-motion fallback and deferred-until-dialog-close behavior. Device-local acknowledgments suppress repeat ceremonies after corrections; the durable shelf comes from saved practice, not browser storage. No sound.

## Latest interaction preferences
Monthly editions are surprises: Preferences has no edition picker, preview/apply button, or preview banner. Calendar-based automatic selection and the internal QA presentation hook remain. This supersedes the earlier user-facing preview description. Secondary action buttons use a theme-derived background, visible 1px boundary, rounded corners, 44px minimum height, explicit hover/focus/disabled states, and no underline; this includes shelf/history/edit/remove and demo return actions.

## Big Dreams home and mobile galleries
Big Dreams are named project/category groupings, each with its own section heading, active/completed count, and Organizar button. Creation and editing use one dialog with a name and labeled member checkboxes; selecting a member from elsewhere moves it, and removing a Big Dream preserves all dreams. The dream form has an optional Big Dream selector. Membership is persisted per owner and unrelated to reward eligibility.

Home cards use 16–18px padding, 21px titles, 36px countdowns, smaller live edition amulets, and 44px daily/history controls. Desktop has a three-column compact grid, two columns below 1000px. At 560px and below each grouping becomes a swipe gallery: horizontal overflow inside the section, start-aligned scroll snapping, hidden scrollbar and a 24px next-card affordance. One card fills its row. The region is keyboard focusable and named for its Big Dream. These rules apply across all monthly editions and override their former oversized card placement, not their illustration or motion.
